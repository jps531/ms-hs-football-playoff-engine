How it works

Every number on this site comes from playing out the rest of a region’s schedule, every possible way when they can be counted and 50,000 simulated ways when they can’t, then applying the MHSAA’s own tiebreaker rules to each outcome. Nothing is hand-picked, and nothing is a pundit’s opinion.

Every remaining game, every way it could go

A region with six games left has 64 ways those games can finish. We build the final standings for each one, break any ties, and count how often each team lands in each seed. A team that finishes first in 16 of the 64 has a 25% toss-up chance at the region title.

Early in the season, when a region has more than 15 games left and there are too many outcomes to list, we simulate the rest of the schedule 50,000 times instead: once using each team’s Elo rating to set its chance of winning each game, for Projected odds, and once with every game a coin flip, for Toss-up odds.

Projected and toss-up odds

Every outcome can be counted two ways, and you can switch between them at the top of each page.

Projected weights each outcome by how likely it is. Every team carries an Elo rating built from its results, with extra credit for margin of victory and a carryover from last season, and those ratings, plus home-field advantage, set each team’s chance of winning each remaining game. A 70% favorite’s win counts for more than its upset loss.

Toss-up treats every remaining game as a coin flip, so each outcome counts the same. It describes the shape of the race rather than who’s better: how many of the possible endings put a team in, regardless of how likely any one game is.

Clinched and eliminated mean the same thing in both. A team that’s in no matter what is 100% either way.

How the Elo ratings work

Each team’s Elo rating is a single number built from its game results. Beating a team raises a rating and losing lowers it, by more when the result is a surprise and, with diminishing returns, by more for a bigger margin. Region games move ratings a little more than other games.

Ratings start each season from a class baseline, 1000 in 1A rising by 50 per class to 1300 in 7A, and teams with a previous season start halfway between that baseline and where they finished last year. The home team gets a 65-point boost. A 100-point edge on a neutral field works out to about a 64% chance to win; a 200-point edge, about 76%.

A rating is a number, not a ranking: it says how strong the results suggest a team is, and it’s only used here to set the chances of individual games for Projected odds. You can see a team’s rating by opening its row in the standings.

Breaking ties the way the MHSAA does

When teams finish with the same region record, they’re separated in this order, following the region tiebreaker procedure in the 2026–27 MHSAA Handbook:

  1. Head-to-head results among the tied teams.
  2. Results against the highest-finishing teams in the region, working down. Teams with the same region record count as one when the tied teams’ results against them are compared.
  3. Point differential in region games among the tied teams, capped at 12 points per game.
  4. Point differential, capped at 12, in region games against the highest-finishing teams, compared the same way.
  5. Fewest points allowed in all region games.
  6. A coin flip.

Because steps 3, 4 and 5 depend on the score, not just the winner, some races turn on margin of victory or points allowed. Once five or fewer region games remain, we work through every winning margin as well, which is why a scenario can read “Raleigh beats Magee by 8–10.” With more games left, the odds work out such a tie as if each remaining game were won by 7 points, so they’re close but not exact. Each margin counts as often as it happened in 2025’s region games: across all of them for Toss-up odds, and among games where the winner had a similar chance for Projected odds. In 2025, a heavy favorite won by 12 or more over nine times in ten; a coin-flip game was won by that much about half the time. When a tie comes down to points allowed with a tied team still to play, the odds try the remaining games at scores drawn from 2025’s region games. While games remain, a tie that would come down to a coin flip counts each way the flip could go equally. Once region play is over, a team whose tie can only be settled by a coin flip is marked with a coin.

What the scenarios mean

Once five or fewer region games remain, each team’s possible outcomes are listed with exactly what has to happen. Conditions joined by AND must all happen together. When there’s more than one way to get there, the alternatives are separated by OR, and any one of them is enough. When every alternative shares some conditions, those come first, and the alternatives follow in a box headed AND ONE OF. The Scenarios section lists every way the region can finish, one box each, with its chance in the odds mode you’ve picked; together they add up to 100% (give or take rounding).

With six to ten games left, we show only results that settle a spot outright, no matter how every other game turns out, checked against every possible winning margin. The headline at the top of each region is drawn from these same proven facts.

What “clinched” and “eliminated” mean

Clinched means a team makes the playoffs, or locks in a specific seed, in every possible outcome of the remaining games. Eliminated means it misses the playoffs in every one. We show 0% and 100% only for those certainties; anything short of that reads as less than 1% or more than 99%.

How the bracket works

The bracket page follows the MHSAA’s playoff bracket for each class. Every first-round spot belongs to a region seed (Region 3 #1 Seed, for example), and every later game is fed by the winners of two earlier ones. A team’s chance to play in a game is its chance of finishing in a seed that feeds that game, combined with its chance of winning every game on the way there. Once a playoff game is played, its winner is fixed.

In Toss-up odds, every playoff game is a coin flip. In Projected odds, a playoff game’s chances come from Elo ratings: for each seed, the ratings of the teams that could finish there, weighted by how likely each one is to get it, with the better seed treated as the home team.

A matchup’s chance is the chance of that exact game happening. Two teams from the same region are handled together, using how often the region’s possible finishes put each in its seed, so a matchup never pairs teams in seeds the standings can’t give them at the same time. Pairings under 0.5% in both odds modes aren’t listed; each game says how many it leaves out.

Who hosts follows the MHSAA’s rules. When two teams from the same region meet, the higher seed always hosts. Otherwise, the higher seed hosts in the first round (and the second round, in 1A–4A); in the quarterfinals, the team that has played fewer home playoff games hosts, then the higher seed; and in the semifinals, the North and South championships, the higher seed hosts. Equal seeds from different regions go to the lower-numbered region in odd years and the higher-numbered region in even years. The state championship is played at a neutral site.

Rules, not AI

No AI model produces any number, scenario, or headline on this site. Every result comes from fixed, written-down rules: counting every way the remaining games can go, applying the MHSAA tiebreakers to each one, and, for Projected odds, Elo ratings computed from game results. Even the sentence at the top of each region is assembled from those proven facts by a template, not written by a language model.

Given the same games, the rules give the same answer every time. That includes the early-season simulation: it’s seeded from the games themselves, so the same games always produce the same simulated runs and the same odds, and the numbers only change when a result does. AI coding tools helped build the website and its code; they play no part in calculating the results.

Where the scores come from

Schedules and scores come from AHSFHS, the source of truth this site relies on for Mississippi high school football results. Region and classification assignments come from the MHSAA. When a score is wrong at the source, it’s wrong here until it’s corrected.

When it updates

Every morning at 9:00 AM Central, the latest scores are pulled. As soon as that finishes, the standings, odds, scenarios, and playoff brackets are recalculated, which can take from a few minutes to about an hour. Results from a Friday night show up Saturday morning. The line under each region’s headline says which week the numbers cover, and the bottom of each page says when they were calculated.

What we don’t do

We don’t publish point spreads, predicted scores, or betting lines, and we don’t pick winners. Every percentage on this site is the chance of something in the standings or the bracket: a seed, a finish, a playoff spot, a home game, a matchup, or a round reached. Seeds aren’t official until the MHSAA announces them.

What the numbers can’t see

The odds know only the schedule, the scores, and the rules. They don’t account for:

  • Injuries, suspensions, or other changes to who’s playing.
  • Weather, travel, or anything else that happens on game day.
  • Forfeits, score corrections, or schedule changes until they’re recorded at the source, or reported to us and approved.

Early in the season, ratings rest on a class baseline, last year’s results, and only a few games, so Projected odds say less in September than they do in late October. High school seasons are short, and a single game can swing a race. The model hasn’t yet been checked against outside benchmarks; we’re keeping a record of every published number so its accuracy can be measured after the season.

A 70% chance of finishing first means the team finishes first in 70% of the ways the season can play out, counted the way the odds mode describes. It isn’t a guarantee.

Not for gambling

The odds on this site are statistical estimates for information and entertainment only. They’re meant to help fans understand how a playoff race might unfold. Do not use them to place bets or wagers of any kind.

What’s next
